Netanyahu calculates that alienating Trump is the least damaging option available to him.

By the EcstacyGlobal team · 8 min read

On difficult issues, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s instinct is usually to delay making a decision. When he does have to take a position, he tends to leave himself some ambiguity and room to manoeuvre, just in case circumstances become more complicated later. This time, however, he has taken a different approach—particularly when it comes to US President Donald Trump’s roadmap for advancing the Gaza ceasefire agreement. .

“I want to be clear: Israel does not accept the 15-point document,” Netanyahu said in his opening remarks at Sunday’s weekly Israeli Cabinet meeting.

In fact, Netanyahu went a step further and repeated the point twice.

“I’ve heard people saying, ‘You didn’t say it.’ So let me say it again: Israel does not accept the 15-point document.”

2. Build a small buffer first

Investing before you have any safety net means you'll likely be forced to sell at the worst possible time — the moment an emergency hits. A small buffer, even one month of essential expenses, changes the entire equation. It means market drops become something you can ignore rather than something that forces your hand.

3. Start with the boring option

The instinct for a first investment is often to chase something exciting — a hot stock, a new coin. The evidence consistently favors the boring choice: a broad, diversified, low-cost fund. It won't make for an exciting story, but it's the option that actually tends to work over long periods.

  • Consistency beats timing — investing the same small amount regularly outperforms trying to guess the right moment
  • Fees matter more than most people think — a high-fee fund can quietly erase years of gains
  • Time in the market matters more than the amount you start with

4. Automate it so willpower isn't required

The most reliable investors aren't the ones with the best predictions — they're the ones who removed the decision entirely by automating a fixed transfer the day they're paid. Willpower is a limited resource; automation doesn't run out.
